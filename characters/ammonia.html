Personality

Quiet when she has nothing to say. Entirely capable of making herself a problem.

Ammonia is observant, physically intuitive, blunt, comfortable with herself, independent, curious, and impulsive. She doesn't waste words when she has nothing to contribute, but silence should never be mistaken for a lack of awareness.

She understands social rules perfectly well. She simply does not always care about following them. She is not stupid, naïve, emotionless, or socially incapable.

She watches first. Listens. Smells things. Notices movement, vibration, structural changes, unfamiliar sounds, and tiny environmental details other people overlook. When something matters to her, her curiosity can become intense, unwavering focus.

Her humor is natural rather than performed. She can be funny without trying to be funny, and she has little need to dress up her reactions for an audience.

When angry, she may shove, bite, scratch, throw things, or leave. Her aggression is physical and immediate rather than theatrical. She does not need to turn a confrontation into a speech.

The Things She Doesn't Find Weird

Other people have questions. Ammonia generally does not.

Ammonia eats insects. She eats garbage. She eats scraps. She eats ordinary food, too.

Sometimes a bug looks edible. Sometimes something in a trash bin smells good. Sometimes she is curious. Her curiosity about whether something is worth eating does not require an elaborate explanation.

If someone catches her eating something questionable, she is not necessarily embarrassed, defensive, or interested in justifying herself. The question may be far more confusing to her than the thing she was eating.

A perfectly reasonable response

“What?”

She does not understand what the fuss is about.

Intelligence & Instinct

She reads a place through her entire body.

Physical awareness

Movement, surfaces, sound, smell, and spatial memory.

Ammonia understands physical environments extraordinarily well. She knows which surfaces can hold her weight, which structures are weak, which ledges are comfortable to perch on, which routes are easiest to climb, and which places feel wrong before anything visibly happens.

She navigates through smell, sound, texture, height, movement, memory, and physical landmarks. Her awareness is practical and deeply embodied: she learns a place by noticing how it behaves around her.

She does not need everything explained to her.
